A Russian verb has a dictionary form (the **infinitive**, usually ending in -ть) and changes its ending for person, number, tense and, in the past, gender.

## Present tense: two conjugations

Most verbs follow one of two patterns (спряжения), and the **ты** form tells you which.

- **First conjugation** (I): ты form in -ешь or -ёшь, они form in -ют or -ут.
- **Second conjugation** (II): ты form in -ишь, они form in -ят or -ат.

## Past tense: by gender, not person

The past tense ends in -л, -ла, -ло, -ли. It agrees with the **gender and number** of the subject, not with the person: я читал (a man speaking), я читала (a woman speaking).

::sentence verb читать past_f

## Future tense

Perfective verbs have no present tense. Their "present" endings give the **future**: сделаю means "I will do".

::table verb сделать

Imperfective verbs form the future with буду, будешь… + the infinitive (буду читать). See the aspect page for when to use which.

## The imperative

The imperative (повелительное наклонение) gives an instruction. The ты form usually ends in -й, -и or -ь; add -те for вы.
