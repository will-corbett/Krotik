An adjective takes the **gender, number and case** of the noun it describes. In the plural, gender no longer matters: one set of endings serves all three genders.

## The endings

Most adjectives follow this pattern. In the plural every gender uses the same column.

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | -ый / -ой | -ая | -ое | -ые |
| Genitive | -ого | -ой | -ого | -ых |
| Dative | -ому | -ой | -ому | -ым |
| Accusative | like nom. or gen. | -ую | -ое | like nom. or gen. |
| Instrumental | -ым | -ой | -ым | -ыми |
| Prepositional | -ом | -ой | -ом | -ых |

In the accusative, masculine and plural follow the noun's **animacy**: like the nominative for things, like the genitive for people and animals.

## Spelling rules

After г, к, х, ж, ш, щ and ч you write **и**, not ы, and after ж, ш, щ, ч and ц an unstressed **е**, not о:

## Common mistakes

| Not this | But this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| интересный книга | интересная книга | **Wrong.** The adjective takes the noun's gender: книга is feminine. |
| в новый доме | в новом доме | **Wrong.** The adjective takes the noun's case too. |
| Я вижу нового стол. | Я вижу новый стол. | **Wrong.** стол is inanimate, so its accusative is like the nominative. Only animate masculine nouns take the genitive form. |
