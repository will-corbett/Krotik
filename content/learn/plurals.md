The plural is where Russian nouns get interesting. The nominative plural has a few patterns and a group of irregular nouns; the genitive plural is the hardest form of all, and it comes up all the time after numbers and quantity words.

## Nominative plural

| Ending | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -ы | masculine and feminine, hard stems | столы, страны |
| -и | soft stems, and after г, к, х, ж, ш, щ, ч | ночи, руки, книги |
| -а / -я | neuter nouns | места, моря |
| stressed -а | some masculine nouns | дома, города, глаза |
| -ья | some masculine and neuter nouns | друзья, братья, деревья |

A few are completely irregular: человек → люди, ребёнок → дети.

## Genitive plural

- **-ов / -ев** for most masculine nouns: столов, городов;
- **-ей** for nouns ending in a soft sign or ж, ш, щ, ч: ночей, ключей;
- **no ending** for most feminine nouns in -а and neuter nouns in -о: книг, мест, слов. A vowel often appears to split two final consonants.

::table noun место

## After numbers

- **1** (and 21, 31…, but not 11): nominative singular: один стол.
- **2, 3, 4** (and 22–24…, but not 12–14): genitive singular: два стола.
- **5 and up**, and **11–14**: genitive plural: пять столов.

## After quantity words

много, мало, несколько and сколько take the **genitive plural** for things you can count (много книг) and the **genitive singular** for things you can't (много времени).
