Russian shows how formal you are more plainly than English: in **ты** or **вы**, in how you name people, and in the
words you choose. Getting it wrong isn't a grammar mistake, but it can sound rude, cold or stiff.

## ты or вы

Use **вы** to anyone you don't know, to older people, and at work, unless you've agreed otherwise. Use **ты** with
family, friends and children, and between young people. Moving from вы to ты is a step people agree on, and the one
who is older or senior usually offers it:

::tatoeba 13804199 13804156

Вы to one person still takes a plural verb and plural short adjectives: Вы готовы? Вы устали? A full adjective stays
singular: Вы такой добрый. In letters, Вы to one person is often written with a capital.

## Names

At work, with teachers and with older people, Russians use the first name and patronymic (имя и отчество, as in Ivan
Petrovich). Friends use a short form of the first name (Sasha for Aleksandr). The surname alone sounds official or
like a school register.

## Greeting, thanking, saying goodbye

| Formal | Everyday |
|---|---|
| Здравствуйте! | Привет! |
| До свидания! | Пока! |
| Благодарю вас. | Спасибо! |

Спасибо is fine anywhere; благодарю is for formal letters and speeches.

## Asking politely

An imperative with пожалуйста is polite enough for most requests. To be more careful, ask a question with **не могли
бы вы** (*could you*), or start with **будьте добры** (*would you be so kind*):

## Bookish and everyday words

Formal writing has its own words for everyday ones. In speech they sound stiff; in a report the everyday ones sound
careless.

| Everyday | Bookish |
|---|---|
| быть | являться |
| делать | осуществлять |
| этот | данный |
| потому что | так как, поскольку |
| сейчас | в настоящее время |

## Common mistakes

| Not this | But this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Привет! (to a teacher) | Здравствуйте! | Unnatural: привет is for friends. |
| Ты не мог бы открыть окно? (to a stranger) | Вы не могли бы открыть окно? | Correct, but rude: strangers get вы. |
| Вы готов? | Вы готовы? | Wrong: a short adjective after вы is plural, even for one person. |
| Мой брат является студентом. (in conversation) | Мой брат студент. | Correct, but bookish: являться belongs in writing. |
