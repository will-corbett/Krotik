Verbs of motion (глаголы движения) come in pairs where English has a single verb. Both verbs of a pair are imperfective; the difference is the **direction**.

## One direction, or not

- **Unidirectional** (однонаправленный): one trip, in one direction, often in progress right now. *I'm walking to school.*
- **Multidirectional** (разнонаправленный): round trips, repeated or habitual trips, movement with no set direction, or the ability to move. *I walk to school every day. Children run around the garden.*

| Meaning | One direction | Multidirectional |
|---|---|---|
| go on foot | идти | ходить |
| go by vehicle | ехать | ездить |
| run | бежать | бегать |
| fly | лететь | летать |
| swim, sail | плыть | плавать |
| carry | нести | носить |
| lead | вести | водить |
| transport | везти | возить |

## The pair in full

Many unidirectional verbs have an irregular stem: идти gives иду, шёл.

## In real sentences

One direction, in progress:

::sentence verb идти pf_sg1

A round trip or habit:

::sentence verb ходить past_m

> Walking versus going by vehicle matters in Russian: идти and ходить are only for going on foot. For any trip by car, bus or train, use ехать or ездить.
