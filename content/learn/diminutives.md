Diminutives (уменьшительно-ласкательные формы) make a noun smaller, or more affectionate. They are everywhere in everyday Russian, from talking to children to ordering a coffee.

## The common suffixes

| Gender | Suffixes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | -ик, -чик, -ок, -ек | дом → домик, сын → сынок |
| Feminine | -ка, -очка, -ечка, -ушка | рука → ручка, мама → мамочка |
| Neuter | -ко, -ышко, -ечко | слово → словечко, солнце → солнышко |

The gender always stays the same as the original noun. A stem consonant often changes before the suffix: рука → ручка, нога → ножка.

## Not just "small"

A diminutive can mean:

- **small**: домик ("a little house");
- **affection**: мамочка, сынок;
- **politeness or softening**: минутку! ("just a minute!");
- **a different thing altogether**: ручка means a pen or a door handle, not just a small hand.

> There's no rule for which suffix a noun takes, and many nouns have several diminutives. The drill accepts any diminutive listed in Wiktionary.
