Almost every Russian verb comes as a pair: an **imperfective** (несовершенный вид) and a **perfective** (совершенный вид). Both mean the same action; the aspect (вид) says how you look at it.

## What each aspect says

| Imperfective | Perfective |
|---|---|
| the action as a process: *was reading* | one complete action with a result: *read (and finished)* |
| repeated or habitual actions: *used to read, reads every day* | a single occasion |
| the fact of the action, not its result: *Have you ever read it?* | one of a sequence: *read it, then left* |
| present, past and future (буду читать) | past and future only |

> A quick test: if the result matters, or the action happens once and is completed, use the perfective. If it's ongoing, repeated, or you only care that it happened at all, use the imperfective.

## How pairs are formed

There is no single rule. You need to learn each pair. The common patterns are:

- **a prefix** makes the perfective: делать → сделать, читать → прочитать, писать → написать;
- **a suffix** makes the imperfective: показать → показывать, решить → решать;
- **a different stem**: говорить → сказать, брать → взять.
