Idioms are fixed phrases whose meaning isn't the sum of their words: **вешать лапшу на уши** is literally *to hang
noodles on someone's ears*, and means *to pull someone's leg*. Proverbs are whole sayings: **куй железо, пока горячо**,
*strike while the iron is hot*. Russians use both often, in conversation, in the news and in jokes.

Every idiom and proverb here comes from Wiktionary, and each is used in at least one real sentence: press the arrow
beside it to see one.

## Using them

- An idiom with a verb changes like any verb: вешать лапшу на уши, but Не вешай мне лапшу на уши!
- Many are colloquial. In a formal letter, keep to plain words.
- Proverbs are often only half said, as in English: Куй железо… is enough.

## Common mistakes

| Not this | But this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Идёт дождь кошками и собаками. | Льёт как из ведра. | Wrong: an English idiom translated word for word means nothing in Russian. |
| Он вешает лапшу на уши. | Он вешает нам лапшу на уши. | Unnatural: you hang the noodles on *someone's* ears. |
