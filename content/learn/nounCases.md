Russian nouns change their ending to show what job they do in a sentence. These forms are the **cases** (падежи). There are six, and each answers its own question word.

## The six cases at a glance

| Case | Russian | Question | Main uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | именительный | кто? что? | the subject; the dictionary form |
| Genitive | родительный | кого? чего? | "of"; after нет; after numbers and quantity words; after у, без, из, от, до, для |
| Dative | дательный | кому? чему? | the person you give or say something to; after к and по |
| Accusative | винительный | кого? что? | the direct object; direction after в and на |
| Instrumental | творительный | кем? чем? | the tool or means; "together with" after с; after над, под, перед, между |
| Prepositional | предложный | о ком? о чём? где? | after о ("about"); location after в and на |

> The question words are worth learning: asking yourself *кому?* or *чем?* is the quickest way to find the case a sentence needs.

A masculine noun ending in a consonant:

::table noun стол

Notice that for **стол** the accusative singular is the same as the nominative. That is the rule for masculine nouns that are *inanimate* (things).

## Animate nouns in the accusative

For masculine nouns that are *animate* (people and animals), the accusative singular looks like the genitive. In the plural this applies to animate nouns of every gender.

::table noun друг
