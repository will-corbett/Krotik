Russian speakers use idioms and proverbs all the time. Many come from fables, folk tales and classic books, so they are worth learning as whole phrases.

## Idioms you'll hear every week

| Idiom | Word for word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ни пуха ни пера! | neither fluff nor feather | Good luck! (answer: К чёрту!) |
| вешать лапшу на уши | hang noodles on someone's ears | to tell someone lies |
| как две капли воды | like two drops of water | exactly alike |
| как рыба в воде | like a fish in water | completely at ease |
| когда рак на горе свистнет | when the crayfish whistles on the hill | never |
| руки не доходят | the hands don't reach | I never get round to it |
| делать из мухи слона | make an elephant out of a fly | make a mountain out of a molehill |
| бить в глаза | hit the eyes | to be obvious |
| не в своей тарелке | not in one's own plate | ill at ease |
| на седьмом небе | in the seventh heaven | very happy |

## Proverbs

- Без труда не вытащишь и рыбку из пруда. (*Without effort you can't even pull a fish out of a pond.*) Nothing comes without work.
- Тише едешь — дальше будешь. (*The slower you go, the further you get.*) More haste, less speed.
- Не всё то золото, что блестит. All that glitters is not gold.
- Лучше поздно, чем никогда. Better late than never.
- Старый друг лучше новых двух. (*One old friend is better than two new ones.*)
- Не откладывай на завтра то, что можно сделать сегодня. Don't put off till tomorrow what you can do today.
- Утро вечера мудренее. (*The morning is wiser than the evening.*) Sleep on it.
- Аппетит приходит во время еды. Appetite comes with eating.

> Proverbs are often cut short: people say just Без труда… and expect you to know the rest.

## Using them

Idioms are informal: use them with friends, not in a job interview. Proverbs are fine almost anywhere, and quoting one at the right moment is always appreciated.
