Food is at the centre of hospitality. A guest is fed, whatever the hour, and refusing everything can seem unfriendly.

## Meals

| Meal | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| завтрак | morning | porridge (каша), eggs, bread with cheese or sausage, tea or coffee |
| обед | early afternoon | the main meal: soup first, then a main course |
| ужин | evening | a lighter hot meal |

Soup (суп) at lunch is normal every day: борщ, щи, суп с лапшой. In Kazakhstan and Central Asia, плов and лагман are everyday dishes too. Bread (хлеб) comes with almost every meal, including soup.

## Everyday dishes

- **каша**: porridge of buckwheat, oats, rice or semolina, eaten at breakfast or as a side dish.
- **пельмени**: small dumplings filled with meat, boiled and served with sour cream (сметана).
- **блины**: thin pancakes, sweet or savoury.
- **салат**: often with mayonnaise; for celebrations, many kinds at once.
- **котлеты**: meat patties, with mashed potato or buckwheat.

Sour cream, dill and other fresh herbs go on almost everything.

## Tea

Tea (чай) comes with almost everything, and with something sweet: jam (варенье), sweets (конфеты), biscuits (печенье). Давай попьём чаю means *let's sit down and talk*, not only *let's drink tea*.

> Notice чаю here: the partitive (Special case forms), *some tea*.

## At the table

- Before eating: Приятного аппетита! (*Enjoy your meal!*)
- To the host: Спасибо, всё очень вкусно!
- When you've had enough: Спасибо, я сыт (or сыта, if you're a woman).
- Asking for something: Передайте, пожалуйста, хлеб.
- Offering: Возьмите ещё! (*Have some more!*)

A festive meal (застолье) is long, with many small dishes (салаты, закуски) on the table at once, and toasts between them. Hot dishes come later, and dessert with tea comes last, sometimes hours after the start.

## Eating out

In a café or restaurant you call the waiter with Извините! or Будьте добры! and ask for the bill with Счёт, пожалуйста. Splitting the bill is common among young people; otherwise one person often pays for everyone.
