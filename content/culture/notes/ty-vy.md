Russian has two words for *you*: ты for one person you know well, and вы, which is both the plural and the polite form for one person.

## Who gets ты, who gets вы

- **ты**: family, friends, children, and usually people your own age in informal settings.
- **вы**: strangers, older people, teachers, doctors, shop staff, colleagues you don't know well.

When in doubt, use вы. Being too formal is a small mistake; being too familiar can sound rude.

> In letters, a capital Вы is a mark of respect to one person: Спасибо Вам!

## The verb agrees

вы always takes the plural verb, even for one person: Вы знаете? Вы были правы. A short adjective is plural too (Вы правы), but a long adjective agrees with the real person: Вы такой добрый (to a man), Вы такая добрая (to a woman).

## Switching to ты

Moving from вы to ты is a small social step, usually suggested by the older or more senior person. People say Давай на ты (*let's say ты*), or ask Можно на ты?

## Imperatives

The two forms have different imperatives: скажи to a friend, скажите to a stranger or to several people. Пожалуйста makes either polite.
