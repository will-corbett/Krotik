Russian has two words for *you*: ты for one person you know well, and вы, which is both the plural and the polite form for one person.

## Who gets ты, who gets вы

- **ты**: family, friends, children, pets, and usually people your own age in informal settings.
- **вы**: strangers, older people, teachers, doctors, shop staff, officials, and colleagues you don't know well.

When in doubt, use вы. Being too formal is a small mistake; being too familiar can sound rude. Young people in a café or at a party may use ты with each other straight away; at work or with anyone older, wait to be invited.

> In letters and messages, a capital Вы is a mark of respect to one person: Спасибо Вам!

## The verb agrees

вы always takes the plural verb, even for one person: Вы знаете? Вы были правы. A short adjective is plural too (Вы правы), but a long adjective agrees with the real person: Вы такой добрый (to a man), Вы такая добрая (to a woman).

## Switching to ты

Moving from вы to ты is a small social step, usually suggested by the older or more senior person. People say Давай на ты (*let's say ты*), or ask Можно на ты? Accepting is friendly; refusing is unusual.

Once you've switched, going back to вы sounds cold, as if something has gone wrong between you.

## Imperatives and greetings

The two forms change other words too:

| With ты | With вы |
|---|---|
| Привет! | Здравствуйте! |
| Как дела? | Как вы поживаете? |
| скажи | скажите |
| садись | садитесь |
| Пока! | До свидания! |

Пожалуйста makes either polite: Скажите, пожалуйста…

## Talking to children and animals

Everyone uses ты to children and animals, and children use ты to their parents and grandparents. Adults they don't know get вы.
