Every Russian has three names: a first name (имя), a patronymic (отчество) made from the father's first name, and a surname (фамилия). Anton Pavlovich Chekhov is Антон Павлович Чехов: Антон, son of Павел, from the Чехов family.

On forms and documents they usually come surname first: Чехов Антон Павлович. In conversation the order is first name, patronymic, surname, and the surname is often left out.

## The patronymic

The patronymic comes from the father's name, with one ending for sons and another for daughters:

| Father | Son | Daughter |
|---|---|---|
| Иван | Иванович | Ивановна |
| Пётр | Петрович | Петровна |
| Павел | Павлович | Павловна |

Names ending in **-й** take **-евич** / **-евна** instead (from Сергей, for example, the son's patronymic ends in -евич).

## Name and patronymic: the polite form

Name and patronymic together (Анна Ивановна, Иван Петрович) is the polite way to address someone older, a teacher, a doctor or a boss. It always goes with вы. At work, at university and with older neighbours you will hear it all the time; among friends and family, almost never.

There is no everyday equivalent of *Mr* or *Mrs*. Господин and госпожа exist, but sound very official; in daily life the name and patronymic do that job.

> Among older colleagues or neighbours, the patronymic alone is sometimes used as a friendly nickname: Петрович. It sounds warm and a little old-fashioned.

## One name, many forms

Most first names have an everyday short form, and that has warmer forms of its own:

| Full name | Everyday form | Affectionate |
|---|---|---|
| Александр, Александра | Саша | |
| Мария | Маша | Машенька |
| Екатерина | Катя | Катенька |
| Михаил | Миша | Мишенька |
| Елена | Лена | Леночка |
| Наталья | Наташа | |
| Николай | Коля | |
| Алексей | Лёша, Алёша | |

The full name sounds formal or official; the short form is what friends and family use. Saying Машенька to a colleague would be strange, but to a child it is normal. A teacher may call a pupil by the short form; the pupil answers with the teacher's name and patronymic.

> The short forms are diminutives (уменьшительно-ласкательные формы), like the nouns in the Diminutives topic.

## Surnames

Surnames in -ов, -ев and -ин have a feminine form in -а: Чехов, but Анна Чехова. They also change for case: письмо Чехову, книга Чехова.

Surnames ending in other letters, or borrowed from other languages, often don't change at all, and Kazakh, Georgian or Armenian surnames follow their own patterns. If in doubt, it's polite to ask how someone's name is said and written.
