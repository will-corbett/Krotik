Every Russian has three names: a first name (имя), a patronymic (отчество) made from the father's first name, and a surname (фамилия). Anton Pavlovich Chekhov is Антон Павлович Чехов: Антон, son of Павел, from the Чехов family.

## The patronymic

The patronymic comes from the father's name, with one ending for sons and another for daughters:

| Father | Son | Daughter |
|---|---|---|
| Иван | Иванович | Ивановна |
| Пётр | Петрович | Петровна |

Name and patronymic together (Анна Ивановна) is the polite way to address an older person, a teacher or a boss. It goes with вы. At work and at school you will hear it all the time; among friends, never.

## One name, many forms

Most first names have a short everyday form, and that has warmer forms of its own:

| Full name | Everyday form | Affectionate |
|---|---|---|
| Александр, Александра | Саша | |
| Мария | Маша | Машенька |
| Екатерина | Катя | Катенька |
| Михаил | Миша | Мишенька |
| Елена | Лена | Леночка |

The full name sounds formal or official; the short form is what friends and family use. Saying Машенька to a colleague would be strange, but to a child it is normal.

> The short forms are diminutives (уменьшительно-ласкательные формы), like the nouns in the Diminutives topic.

## Surnames change too

Surnames in -ов, -ев and -ин have a feminine form in -а: Чехов, but Анна Чехова. They also change for case: письмо Чехову, книга Чехова.
