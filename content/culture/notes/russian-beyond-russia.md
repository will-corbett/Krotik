Russian is spoken by many millions of people outside Russia: in Kazakhstan and Central Asia, the Caucasus, the Baltic states, Ukraine, Belarus, Israel and in communities around the world. The grammar is the same everywhere; the accent and some words change.

## Kazakhstan

In Kazakhstan the state language is Kazakh (казахский язык), and Russian is also an official language, used in government, business and daily life. Many people move between the two, sometimes in one sentence. In cities, especially Almaty and Astana, Russian is heard everywhere; in the countryside and in the south, Kazakh is more common.

Kazakhstani Russian has its own everyday words, many of them from Kazakh:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| аким | the head of a city or region |
| акимат | the city or regional administration |
| той | a big celebration, such as a wedding |
| бешбармак | the national dish of meat and noodles |
| кумыс | fermented mare's milk |

Kazakh and Russian names sit side by side, and Kazakh first names and surnames are used with Russian grammar where they fit.

## Central Asia

- **Kyrgyzstan**: Russian is an official language alongside Kyrgyz.
- **Uzbekistan and Tajikistan**: Russian is not official, but is widely understood in the cities, and used in business and higher education.
- Across the region, Russian is often the shared language between people of different nationalities.

Food words travel with the dishes: плов, лагман and манты are as familiar in Moscow as in Tashkent.

## The Caucasus, the Baltic states and beyond

- **Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan**: Russian is a foreign language, but many people speak it, and you'll find Russian-language media and channels in each country.
- **The Baltic states**: there are large Russian-speaking communities, especially in Latvia and Estonia, with their own media, theatres and comedy.
- **Israel**: one of the largest Russian-speaking communities outside the former Soviet Union.
- **Germany, the USA and elsewhere**: communities large enough to have their own newspapers and shops.

## Is the Russian different?

Mostly in vocabulary and accent, not grammar. A word may be borrowed from the local language, and stress or intonation can sound different, but someone from Almaty and someone from Riga understand each other without effort.

> Wherever you are, the drills on this site apply: the standard language is shared, and local words are extra vocabulary, not different grammar.
