Russian is spoken by many millions of people outside Russia: across Central Asia and the Caucasus, in Belarus, Ukraine, Moldova and the Baltic states, and in large communities in Israel, Germany, the USA and elsewhere. The grammar is the same everywhere; the accent, some everyday words and the place of Russian in public life change from country to country.

## What changes, and what doesn't

- **Grammar**: the same. Everything you drill on this site works in Almaty, Riga or Haifa.
- **Words**: each country adds its own, often borrowed from the local language: food, places, forms of address.
- **Accent**: vowels, the sound of г and the rhythm of speech can differ, but speakers from different countries understand each other without effort.
- **Status**: in some countries Russian is an official language; in others it's a minority or foreign language, and fewer young people learn it.
