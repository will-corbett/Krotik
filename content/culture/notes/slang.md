Spoken Russian, and especially Russian in messages, is shorter and looser than the textbook. These are the forms you'll meet first.

## Short forms in speech

| Written | Said | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| сейчас | щас | now, in a moment |
| что | чё, чо | what |
| тысяча | тыща | a thousand |
| человек | чел | a person, a guy |
| здравствуйте | здрасте | hello |
| ничего | ничё | nothing; it's fine |

## Fillers

Speakers fill pauses with words that mean little on their own: ну (*well*), вот (*so, there*), как бы (*kind of*), типа (*like*), короче (*anyway, long story short*), это самое (*um, what's-it*).

## Everyday informal words

- **нормально**: fine, OK. Как дела? — Нормально.
- **классно**, **круто**: great, cool.
- **ладно**: OK, all right (agreeing).
- **давай**: let's; OK; bye (Ну, давай!).
- **блин**: a mild swear word, literally *pancake*.

## In messages

- спс for спасибо, пж for пожалуйста.
- ок, норм (*fine, OK*).
- A bracket is a smile: ) or )) or more for a bigger smile, and ( for sad. Many people type these instead of emoji.
- Questions often end with ?? or ?!, and a single full stop at the end of a message can sound cold or annoyed.

> Use these with friends only. In emails, at work, and with people you call вы, write in full.
