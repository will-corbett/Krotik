Some of the largest Russian-speaking communities outside the former Soviet Union are in Israel, Germany and the USA.

## Israel

About a million people from the former Soviet Union have moved to Israel since 1989, and Russian is now the third most common native language there after Hebrew and Arabic. There are Russian newspapers, television, shops and theatres, and many offices give information in Russian too.

Israeli Russian borrows everyday words from Hebrew:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| мазган | an air conditioner (from Hebrew) |
| каньон | a shopping mall (in Israel) |
| хумус | hummus |

## Germany

Germany has the largest Russian-speaking population outside the former Soviet Union, about 3 million people: many are ethnic Germans who returned from Russia and Kazakhstan, others are Russians and Jews.

## The USA and elsewhere

In the 2010 census, about 855,000 people in the USA spoke Russian at home, with the best-known community in Brighton Beach in New York. There are also communities in Canada, Australia, Finland and across Europe, with their own shops, papers and schools.
